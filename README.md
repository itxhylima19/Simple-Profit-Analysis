# Simple Profit Analysis

## Project Overview
This Excel project compares profitability across product categories using a Superstore-style dataset. The goal is to understand which categories generate the most profit and how sales, average profit, and profit margin differ by category.

## Objective
- Compare total profit across product categories.
- Understand basic profitability using totals, averages, and profit margin.
- Visualize category-level profit with a chart.

## Tools
- Microsoft Excel

## Dataset
The workbook currently contains a **synthetic Superstore-style practice dataset** with Furniture, Office Supplies, and Technology categories. If the internship requires the official Superstore dataset, replace the Sales Data sheet with the required dataset before final submission.

## Method / Approach
1. Prepared order-level data with Category, Sub-Category, Sales, and Cost.
2. Calculated **Profit = Sales - Cost**.
3. Calculated **Profit Margin = Profit / Sales**.
4. Grouped results by product category using Excel formulas such as `SUMIF` and `AVERAGEIF`.
5. Compared Total Sales, Total Profit, Average Profit, and Profit Margin.
6. Created a chart to compare total profit by category.

## Outcome
In the included practice dataset, Technology has the strongest profit margin, while Furniture has the highest total sales. The analysis demonstrates why high sales do not always mean the highest profitability.

## Interview Questions
**Revenue vs Profit?**  
Revenue is the total money generated from sales. Profit is the amount remaining after costs are deducted.

**Why can sales be high but profit low?**  
High costs, discounts, returns, or low-margin products can cause high sales but low profit.
